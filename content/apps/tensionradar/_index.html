TensionRadar

iPhone · Apple Watch

A little character that feels what your heart feels.

TensionRadar turns the heart data your Apple Watch already records into a live picture of your stress. Heart rate variability arrives the moment the watch writes it to Health, gets scored against your own baseline, and shows up as one of five moods on a cartoon character — on your phone, in a widget, on your wrist and on your watch face.

Version 1.0 submitted to the App Store

What it does

A radar, not a number

Every HRV reading is scored against your personal baseline and blended with how far your heart rate sits above resting. The result is a 0–100 score in five bands: Serene, Calm, Steady, Tense and Overloaded.

Real time, not later

Readings arrive as Apple Watch writes them to Health, and background delivery keeps the radar, widget and watch current even when the app is closed. Newer watches that sample HRV more often make it more responsive still.

Four characters, five moods

Nimbus the cloud is free and breathes, blinks and droops with your data. The optional Character Pack adds Bloop the blob, Mochi the cat and Sprout the plant for a one-time $0.99. Overloaded looks tired, never frightening.

Coherence breathing

Breathe at your resonance frequency with a paced circle. With the watch app measuring, your coherence score climbs in real time, and finished sessions are saved to Health as Mindful Minutes.

Trends and a journal that learns

Daily HRV against your typical band, resting heart rate, HRV by hour of day and a mood calendar of character faces. Tag coffee, sleep or workouts and see which habits move your HRV.

Five watch complications

Your character, your HRV number, a 0–100 stress score, heart rate with resting, and a seven-day HRV chart — in corner, circular, rectangular and inline slots on any watch face, and on the iPhone Lock Screen.

Pick your app icon

Keep the Nimbus radar icon or switch to a character icon in Settings, or let the Home Screen icon follow whichever character you pick.

Alerts on your wrist

When tension rises, your watch shows your character, the stress band and your HRV right in the notification. Every number in the app has a tip explaining what it means.

Privacy first

TensionRadar reads heart data from Apple Health on your device and keeps everything it derives there. No account, no server, no analytics, no third-party SDKs. The only thing it writes back to Health is the breathing sessions you finish. The full story is on the privacy page.

Not a medical device

HRV reflects sleep, training, illness, caffeine, alcohol and stress all at once. TensionRadar is a wellness tool for noticing patterns, not for diagnosing or treating anything.
