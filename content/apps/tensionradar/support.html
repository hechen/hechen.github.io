TensionRadar

Support

Last updated: 22 October 2026

Version scope: The scoring, retained-reading and alert guidance below describes 1.2.1, available on the US App Store; older versions may differ. See How TensionRadar works for the exact formula, defaults and limitations.

Granting Health access

In Health → Sharing → Apps → TensionRadar, review HRV, heart rate, resting heart rate, respiratory rate, beat-to-beat measurements and workout access. Supported systems may also provide RMSSD readings. Workout history helps exclude exercise and recovery. Mindful Minutes writing is optional for finished breathing sessions; workout-sharing permission supports live Watch sessions, whose workout is discarded.

Why does it say “Last reading”?

The most recent accepted result stays visible with its original measurement age when current evidence expires or is unsuitable. It is historical, not a claim about how you feel now. It cannot bypass current alert checks. Without an accepted score, the app can show raw HRV; with no measurement it shows a first-reading state. Older builds may instead show “No Current Reading.”

Does Refresh take a new measurement?

No. It asks for available Health data. Watch sampling, Health availability and background delivery determine when new readings arrive. Compare measurement timestamps in Health and TensionRadar. A more recent heart-rate measurement does not update an older HRV timestamp.

How much history is needed?

A new score needs at least five distinct days of suitable measurements in the matching time-of-day window, separately for SDNN or RMSSD. The default baseline window is 30 days; choose 7, 14, 30 or 60 in Settings. Today is excluded. Population fallback values do not qualify for a new score in 1.2.1.

The character looks Overloaded but I feel fine

The label is a score band, not a diagnosis or a measurement of your emotions. Check the statistic, timestamp and “Why This Reading” explanation. Journal check-ins record how you actually feel. Low HRV has many possible influences; a difference or association does not establish a cause. See the methodology’s limitations before interpreting a change.

Movement and workouts

Activity comes from sampled Health metadata and workout records, not continuous movement tracking or heart rate alone. Workouts and the following ten minutes are excluded from a new resting interpretation. Missing metadata is not proof of rest. The last accepted result can remain visible while waiting for new evidence. Guided-breathing history is not comprehensively filtered from the baseline in this version.

Coherence breathing has no score

Coherence needs live heart data. Choose Apple Watch as the measurement source before you begin; the TensionRadar watch app then samples your heart rate during the session and an estimate becomes available when enough usable data arrives. Without a source, Breathe still works as a paced exercise and is saved to Health as Mindful Minutes.

Adding complications

Long-press your watch face, tap Edit, swipe to Complications, pick a slot and choose TensionRadar. Five kinds are available on compatible faces and slots: Tension Radar (your character with the stress ring), HRV (your latest number against your typical range), Stress Score (0–100), Heart Rate (with resting heart rate) and HRV · 7 Days (a weekly bar chart for large rectangular slots). The same set is available on the iPhone Lock Screen.

Widgets or complications are out of date

TensionRadar requests a widget and complication refresh when it receives a new reading. Apple controls the final refresh timing. Open TensionRadar on your phone and watch to check the latest available data and request a refresh; updates can still be delayed by system scheduling or connectivity.

Compare the measurement time in Apple Health with the time shown in TensionRadar. If Health has no newer reading, a widget refresh cannot create one. Health data can also be unavailable while the phone is locked. After installing an update, launch TensionRadar on both your iPhone and Apple Watch once and confirm Health access.

Calendar widgets in 1.2.1

Version 1.2.1 includes daily-HRV calendar widgets and trend views. The character summarizes daily HRV relative to usual days; it does not show Journal mood check-ins. Days without data remain empty.

Changing the app icon

Settings → App icon lets you choose the Nimbus radar icon or a character icon (character icons other than Nimbus need the Character Pack). Turn on Match my character and the Home Screen icon changes whenever you pick a different character. iOS shows a short confirmation each time the icon changes.

Alerts in 1.2.1

HRV alerts require a fresh eligible reading and a preceding same-statistic reading that confirms the threshold. A separate sustained resting-heart-rate rule also exists. Historical display results cannot trigger current alerts. Default spacing is 15 minutes; choices are 0, 15, 30 or 60 minutes. Threshold choices are 60, 70 or 80. Zero spacing does not remove confirmation, activity or duplicate checks. Morning summaries use a separate opportunistic path and are not guaranteed each day.

Settings → Alerts provides alert hours, delivery-device selection and local diagnostics. iPhone is the default; Watch-local tension alerts require explicit selection and acknowledgment. Morning summaries stay on iPhone. Permission, Focus, Health access and system scheduling can affect delivery. A scheduled notification is not proof that it was displayed or seen. The methodology page gives the exact freshness, confirmation and escalation rules.

Changing the character

Settings → Your character. Nimbus is the default character. The optional Character Pack adds more designs with matching app icons. Each character has five expressions representing score categories, and your choice syncs to the watch app when the devices can communicate. Widgets and complications reflect it when the system refreshes them.

Restoring the Character Pack

The purchase is tied to your Apple Account and shared through Family Sharing. On a new device, open Settings → Purchases → Restore Purchases.
