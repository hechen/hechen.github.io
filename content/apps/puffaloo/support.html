Puffaloo

Support

Last updated: 14 October 2026

Puffaloo is a match-3 puzzle game for iPhone and iPad. Swap neighbouring pals to line up three or more of a kind, and clear each level's goal before you run out of moves. There are 96 levels across eight chapters: Sunny Meadow, Candy Shore, Starlight Snow, Autumn Forest, Coral Reef, Cloud Castle, Honey Hollow and the Crystal Caves. Chapter 1 is free to play; the Full Game purchase unlocks the rest.

How to play

Swap two pals that sit next to each other to make a row or column of three or more of the same kind, or a 2×2 square of four. If the swap doesn't make a match, the pals swap back and no move is used. Matched pals pop, the pals above fall into place, and new ones drop in from the top, which can set off a chain. If the board has no matches left to make, it shuffles itself.

The first levels walk you through each move step by step. If you stop for a few seconds, a hint shows a match you can make.

Special pals

  • Striped Pal: line up four. Match it again and it clears its whole row or column.
  • Bomb Pal: match in an L or T shape. Match it again and it blasts the pals in the area around it.
  • Rainbow Pal: line up five. Swap it with a pal to clear every pal of that kind from the board.
  • Balloon Pal: bring four of the same pal together in a 2×2 square. Match it again and it floats off to pop one useful target: an ice tile first, then a crate, then a pal wrapped in seaweed or covered in honey, then a pal the level still asks you to collect, otherwise any pal.
  • Combos: swap two special pals with each other for a bigger effect than either one alone.

Square matches and the Balloon Pal

Four pals of the same kind in a 2×2 square count as a match, on every level. A square on its own makes a Balloon Pal; if the square is part of a line of three or more, the line decides which special pal you get and the square just adds its pals to the match. Level 4 shows you how it works.

When a Balloon Pal is matched it lifts off, flies to its target and pops it like a one-cell blast: ice and crates lose a layer, a special pal at the target goes off. Swapping a Balloon Pal into another special pal carries that pal's effect to the balloon's target:

  • Balloon + Striped: the striped beams fire from the target, clearing both its row and its column.
  • Balloon + Bomb: a bomb blast goes off at the target.
  • Balloon + Rainbow: every pal of the balloon's kind turns into a balloon; up to six take flight and the rest pop where they are.
  • Balloon + Balloon: two balloons fly to two different targets.

Level goals, ice, crates, seaweed, baby pals, honey and crystals

Each level shows its goal and how many moves you have. A level might ask you to collect a number of certain pals, crack all the ice, break all the crates, free all the pals in seaweed, bring baby pals home, clear all the honey, break all the crystals, or reach a target score. Ice sits under pals on the board: clear the pal on top of it, with a match or a special pal, to crack it. Thick ice has two layers and needs to be cracked twice.

Wooden acorn crates appear in the Autumn Forest. A crate takes up a cell by itself: it can't be swapped, it doesn't fall, and pals rest on top of it. To break one, make a match right next to it (above, below or beside it) or hit it with a special pal's blast. Each hit knocks off one layer; crates with iron bands have two layers and need two hits. A broken crate leaves a gap that pals fall into, and the cells underneath are refilled by pals sliding in from the neighbouring columns.

Seaweed appears in the Coral Reef. A pal wrapped in seaweed can't be swapped, but it still falls with the others and still counts in a match. Make a match that includes it, or hit it with a special pal's blast, and the seaweed tears off and the pal is cleared as usual.

Baby pals appear in Cloud Castle. A baby sleeps on a little cloud: it can't be matched, and special pals and balloons pass over it. Clear the pals below it so it falls, or swap it with a neighbour when that swap makes a match. When a baby reaches the bottom cell of its column (marked by an arrow under the board), it floats home on a balloon. New babies drop in from the top until the level has all the babies it asks for.

Honey appears in Honey Hollow. A pal covered in honey can't be swapped, but it still falls and still counts in a match: match it, or hit it with a special pal's blast, and the honey comes off. Honey spreads: after every move that clears no honey, one pal in honey drips onto a neighbouring pal (never onto ice, special pals, baby pals or pals in seaweed), so the count in the corner can go up. Boosters never make honey spread.

Crystals appear in the Crystal Caves. Like a crate, a crystal takes up a cell by itself, can't be swapped and doesn't fall, and pals slide around it. Unlike a crate, a match right next to a crystal doesn't hurt it: only a blast that reaches its cell does, from a Striped or Bomb Pal, a Balloon Pal, two special pals swapped together, or the Hammer booster. Each hit knocks off one layer, and taller crystals have two. The first Crystal Caves level shows you how.

Daily Puzzle

The "Today's Puzzle" card at the top of the map opens one board a day: the same board for everyone, 20 moves and a score goal. The day changes at midnight UTC. Your first finished try each day counts: its score goes to the Daily leaderboard in Game Center (if you're signed in) and keeps your streak going. You can replay as often as you like to beat your own best of the day. There are no extra moves and no boosters in the Daily Puzzle, so every score is made with the same 20 moves.

Boosters

You earn a booster (a Hammer, a Shuffle or a Swap, chosen at random) for every three stars you collect and for every Daily Puzzle you play; you can hold up to nine of each. Tap one in the tray under the board: the Hammer pops the pal you tap next, the Shuffle rearranges the board, and the Swap exchanges the next two neighbouring pals you tap, even if they don't make a match. None of them costs a move, and they can't be bought. The Hammer can't pop a baby pal, and the Swap can't move a pal in seaweed or honey.

Seasonal festivals

Three times a year a festival opens beside the map, by your device's date: the Winter Festival from December 1 to January 15, the Spring Festival from March 20 to April 30 and the Summer Festival from June 20 to July 31. While one is open, its card appears under the Today's Puzzle card on the map once you've cleared the first three levels. Each festival has twelve free levels: no purchase is needed. They open one after another, and you can use your boosters and the five free extra moves as usual. Festival stars are kept separately: they don't count toward your star total, the leaderboards or new boosters. Clear all twelve levels of a festival for its achievement (Winter Wonderland, Spring Fling, Summer Splash) and an outfit. When the festival ends, its card disappears and your progress waits for next year.

The Toy Factory

The Toy Factory is an optional expansion: 24 levels in a toy workshop, opened from the gear button at the top of the map once you've cleared the first three levels. Its levels have conveyor belts: after every move, each pal on a belt moves one step along it (a pal leaving the end of a straight belt comes back at its start), so look where your match will land. Its levels open one after another and keep their own stars, which don't count toward your star total, the leaderboards or new boosters. The first two levels are free; the other 22 need a one-time purchase that is separate from the Full Game and supports Family Sharing. Clear all 24 for the Master Toymaker achievement.

Wardrobe and outfits

Tap the T-shirt button at the top of the map to open the wardrobe. Each pal has three outfits, and the penguin, the bunny and the frog a fourth: 21 in all, and you earn every one of them by playing: clearing a chapter, collecting stars, playing the Daily Puzzle on several days, pulling off a big chain or combo, or clearing every level of a festival (the penguin's snowy earmuffs in winter, the bunny's blossom wreath in spring, the frog's flower lei in summer). Locked outfits tell you how to earn them. Pick an outfit to put it on; every pal of that kind wears it, on the board too. Outfits you unlock on one device appear on your other devices through iCloud, but what each pal wears is chosen on each device. Outfits are never sold.

Stars

Each level has up to three stars, earned by your score when you clear it: one for clearing the level, two for a good score and three for a great one. Special pals and chains score more. You can replay any level you've unlocked from the map; Puffaloo keeps your best result.

Running out of moves

If you run out of moves before you reach the goal, you can take five extra moves for free, once per attempt. If you still don't make it, try the level again: the goal and your moves start over.

Music, sound and haptics

Pause a level to turn the music or sound effects on or off. These settings are saved on each device. Haptics follow your system settings; if you don't feel them, check System Haptics in Settings › Sounds & Haptics.

App icons

Every update brings a new App Store icon, and you keep the icons of every version you've played. To switch, open the About & Privacy card (the ⓘ button on the title screen, or About & Privacy in the pause menu) and tap Change app icon. It shows the icons you have: the classic icon, plus the icon of every version from the first one you played up to the current one. Tap one to put it on your Home Screen; your iPhone or iPad confirms the change with a short alert of its own. Puffaloo remembers the first version you played, on your device and in your own iCloud account, so the icons you have come back if you reinstall the app, and appear on your other devices too.

Playing on more than one device

Your progress (unlocked levels, stars, best scores, balloon targets popped, achievements earned, your Daily Puzzle streak and best of the day, your boosters and your festival stars) syncs through iCloud between your iPhone and iPad. Both devices need to be signed in to the same Apple Account with iCloud turned on, and need an internet connection to sync. Open Puffaloo on the other device and give it a moment; if you've played on both, Puffaloo keeps the best result for each level. Music and sound settings don't sync. The Full Game purchase comes from the App Store rather than iCloud: it's available on every device signed in with the same Apple Account (see below).

Game Center

Puffaloo has Game Center leaderboards for total stars, levels cleared and the Daily Puzzle, and achievements to collect. If you're signed in to Game Center, Puffaloo signs you in automatically; the Game Center button on the title screen opens your leaderboards and achievements. If you're not signed in, Puffaloo may offer to sign you in on the title screen or the map, never in the middle of a level. You can also sign in at any time in Settings › Game Center. Game Center is optional: everything else in Puffaloo works without it.

Starting over

Puffaloo doesn't have a reset button. Because your progress is saved in iCloud, deleting and reinstalling the app on one device brings it back. To start from level 1, delete Puffaloo from all of your devices, delete its data from iCloud in the Settings app (your name › iCloud), then reinstall. Game Center keeps the scores and achievements you've already earned, and the Full Game stays yours: if the chapters show as locked after reinstalling, tap Restore Purchases on the Full Game card.

The Full Game purchase

What's free, and what does the Full Game include?

Chapter 1, Sunny Meadow (levels 1–12), is free to play, with every mechanic and no limits. Full Game is the purchase for the main map: it unlocks every other chapter on the map, including new chapters added in future free updates. It's a one-time purchase, not a subscription: buy it once and it's yours to keep.

How do I buy it?

Play through chapter 1, then tap the first level of chapter 2 (or any later level you've reached) on the map. Instead of the level's start card you'll see the Full Game card with the price in your App Store currency; tap the unlock button and confirm with the App Store as you would any purchase. Once the purchase goes through, the same level's start card appears and you can carry on. If a purchase needs a parent's approval (Ask to Buy), the card says it's waiting for approval and your chapters unlock as soon as it's approved.

I already bought it. Why are the chapters locked?

Make sure the device is signed in to the App Store with the Apple Account that made the purchase, then tap Restore Purchases on the About & Privacy card (the ⓘ button on the title screen, or About & Privacy in the pause menu) or on the Full Game card (tap the first level of chapter 2, or any later level you've reached, on the map). The same button brings back the Toy Factory, and the Toy Factory screen has its own Restore Purchases link. Puffaloo asks the App Store what this account owns and unlocks everything it finds right away. You never pay twice: buying again with the same account just restores it.

Can my family use my purchase?

Yes. The Full Game and the Toy Factory support Family Sharing. Turn on Purchase Sharing in the Settings app (your name › Family Sharing) and family members can tap Restore Purchases in their own copy of Puffaloo. Each person's progress and stars stay their own.

I reached chapter 2 or 3 before the purchase existed. Do I lose those levels?

No. If you had already unlocked levels beyond chapter 1 in an earlier version of Puffaloo, those levels stay open for you. Only the levels beyond the point you had reached need the Full Game.

Is there anything else to buy?

Only one optional extra: the Toy Factory expansion (see below), which the Full Game doesn't include and which you don't need for the main map. There are no subscriptions, no lives or energy, and nothing for sale that helps you win a level: boosters are only earned by playing. The five extra moves you can take when you run out are free, and there are no ads.

How do I get a refund?

Purchases are handled by Apple, so refunds are too. Go to reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in with the Apple Account that made the purchase, and request a refund for the purchase. Apple decides on refunds under its own terms; I can't issue them myself. If a refund is granted, the chapters (or the Toy Factory's levels) lock again.
