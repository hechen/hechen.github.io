I bought the AYANEO POCKET DMG when I went back to China in March 2026. Nice little gadget. I've installed a lot of games on it. Pretty cool.

## Familiar shape, different hardware

The upright shape is the first thing you notice. Screen at the top, controls underneath, and a bright orange shell on mine. It has the outline of an older handheld, with a stick and touch controls alongside the more familiar buttons.

[AYANEO's launch information](https://store.ayaneo.com/article/851) describes an Android handheld built around the Snapdragon G3x Gen 2 platform. Its [screen specifications](https://www.ayaneo.com/article/830) list a 3.92-inch OLED panel at 1240 × 1080. That fairly square display is an important part of the design, not just another number on the box.

## What makes the format interesting

For games designed around older, squarer screens, an upright handheld feels like a natural fit. Physical buttons also give it a different character from playing everything on a phone's touchscreen.

The nearly square panel is less well matched to widescreen material. Keeping the original proportions means unused space around some games; filling the screen means accepting a crop or a stretch. The best choice depends on the game.

## The tradeoff

An Android handheld gives you flexibility, but setup becomes part of owning it. Installing games, arranging a library, and sorting out controls take more attention than opening a single-purpose console.

I've already put a lot of games on mine. That doesn't mean every game runs perfectly, and I haven't put together a performance test. The appeal so far is simpler: a cool little device with a lot to play, in a shape that feels distinct from my other screens.
