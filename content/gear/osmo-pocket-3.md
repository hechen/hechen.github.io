I bought the Osmo Pocket 3 on March 3, 2026, during my holiday trip back to China. I ordered it from Taobao, a Chinese online marketplace, for about RMB 3,500. It arrived on March 9.

I later sold it on eBay and bought the [Pocket 4P](/gear/osmo-pocket-4p/). These are the photos I took when I was selling it.

## A camera that keeps the kit small

The Pocket 3 combines a camera and a mechanical gimbal in one handheld body. That is the practical appeal: fewer separate pieces to assemble before filming.

[DJI's specifications](https://www.dji.com/osmo-pocket-3/specs) list a 1-inch sensor, a 20mm-equivalent f/2 lens, and a body weight of 179g. It records regular 4K video at up to 60fps and 4K slow motion at 120fps. The rotating screen lets the controls and framing change without adding a separate monitor.

## Where the simplicity helps

The wide lens makes sense for showing a place and what is happening around the camera. The all-in-one shape also offers a different balance from my [Sony α7 V](/gear/sony-a7-v/): less choice of lenses, but fewer decisions about what to pack.

The sale photos show that even a small camera can accumulate accessories. A case, mini tripod, handle attachment, and filters all take space. The camera's weight is useful to know, but it isn't the weight of the whole kit.

## Why an upgrade isn't automatic

A single wide lens is also the limit. If a tighter view is important, the Pocket 4P's second lens is a meaningful difference. If most clips stay wide, the Pocket 3 already covers that kind of framing.

I moved on to the 4P, but selling this one doesn't make it a bad camera. It is still an interesting approach to portable video: a small body, a screen, and a gimbal, all ready to be carried together.
