The Knob Keyboard arrived on September 2, 2025, after ordering it almost eight months earlier. The orange packaging, pale keycaps, and two black knobs make it a very recognizable desk object.

## What makes it different

The official name is Knob / k.no.b.1, designed by Ben Fryc and developed by Work Louder. The [product page](https://knob.design/) lists low-profile mechanical switches, two programmable knobs, and a narrow full-color screen beside the keys.

The controls are what set it apart. A knob can give an adjustment a physical direction: turn a little, change a little. Whether that is useful depends on what gets assigned to it. The screen has room for things such as a timer or custom wallpaper, rather than trying to become a second full monitor.

## Details worth knowing

Work Louder lists aluminum top and bottom enclosures, PBT keycaps, hot-swappable switches, USB-C, and Bluetooth with up to three saved connections. Those are the published features, not a promise that every low-profile switch or keycap set will fit.

Visually, the combination works because the unusual parts stay concentrated on the right. The main typing area remains easy to recognize. The orange keys add contrast without turning every key into a different color.

## The tradeoff

At the $439 price listed on the product page, this is a substantial keyboard purchase. The knobs and screen need to be part of the appeal; someone who only wants a compact place to type has much simpler options.

Low-profile keys also deserve a try before committing. A thin board is easy to like in a photograph, but switch feel and key shape are what your hands meet all day. Design can make a keyboard interesting. It still has to suit the person typing on it.
